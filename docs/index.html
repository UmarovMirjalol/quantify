Which companies are most exposed to technological disruption?

A transparent, reproducible index scoring 13,702 SEC-registered companies on patent activity and startup-ecosystem activity in their technology domains — and a test of whether markets price it before fundamentals.

Average index by industry

Read with care. Patent data cover 3,891 companies — those whose patents could be linked to an SEC registrant. For the remaining firms the index reflects industry-level exposure to startup activity only; a blank patent score means “not linked”, not “zero patents”.
Methodology

How the index is built

Every parameter lives in one configuration file; every company in the universe is kept, and missing data are never treated as zero.

1

Company universe

13,702 SEC registrants: 7,385 currently listed companies plus historical XBRL filers (reducing survivorship bias). Funds and SPAC shells are flagged.

2

Startup ecosystem

19,820 accelerator startups (YC, Techstars, Plug and Play, 500 Global, Antler, Alchemist, EF) tagged into 9 technology categories; activity is mapped to companies via documented SIC rules.

3

Patents

US patent grants (USPTO PatentsView, linked to public firms by Ma, 2025, and to SEC CIKs) for 3,891 companies: log-scaled volume and 3-year momentum, so the largest patent holders do not dominate.

4

Normalization

Within-year percentiles for startup exposure, fixed log anchors for patents, all on 0–100 and rounded to one decimal.

5

Index

Weighted average of available components (default 50/50). Each company-year reports components available and a coverage score.

6

Fundamentals

Revenue, assets, R&D, CAPEX, cash, debt and public float from SEC XBRL, used to test predictive relationships with year, industry and firm fixed effects.

Data

Open and reproducible

The full pipeline — data collection, scoring, validation and regressions — runs with one command. The index is a measurable proxy for technology exposure, not an objective measure of “true” disruption.
